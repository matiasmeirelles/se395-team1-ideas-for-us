# Pain Points and Bottlenecks

IDEAS for Us faces several operational bottlenecks that come from repetitive administrative work, time-consuming writing, and large amounts of information that have to be processed before decisions can be made. Based on Clayton Ferrara’s class visit, the clearest pattern is that leadership time is often pulled into tasks that do not require leadership-level judgment.

One major pain point is administrative busywork. Clayton described spending time on tasks such as recovering a password for a USDA portal, picking up mail, managing receipts, entering data, and working with spreadsheets. He described this kind of work as “noise” because it takes attention away from the more strategic, creative, and relationship-based work that actually moves the organization forward. This suggests an operational inefficiency where valuable staff time is being used on routine tasks that could potentially be streamlined or automated.

Receipt and expense processing is an example of a bottleneck that IDEAS for Us has already partially reduced. Clayton explained that he previously had to manually record location information and enter receipt data into spreadsheets. Now, he can photograph receipts, upload them into Claude, and use AI to organize the information into a spreadsheet and extract geolocation data from the image metadata. This is useful evidence of the type of repetitive work IDEAS wants to automate, although it appears to be less of an unresolved pain point today.

Grant writing remains a stronger current bottleneck. Clayton directly described grant writing as a very time-consuming process. Although IDEAS for Us uses Claude to produce rough drafts, a large amount of human work still remains. Clayton may spend significant time giving the AI detailed context, reviewing drafts, correcting weak language, adapting responses to specific funders, and making sure the final writing reflects the organization’s voice and mission. This means AI has made grant writing faster, but it has not fully removed the workload.

Research and information synthesis is another major bottleneck. Clayton explained that reviewing public information such as town hall minutes, voting records, commissioners, and committee activity could take around 40 hours manually. The time-consuming part is not only finding the information, but also reading, comparing, organizing, and turning it into something useful for decision-making.

One important gap is that the class visit did not clearly identify specific tasks that are regularly forgotten, delayed, or completed inconsistently. That should not be assumed without more evidence.
