# IDEAS for Us: AI Usage

## Decision Framework

Separate tasks into two categories:

1. **Utilitarian tasks** (AI handles): Data entry, receipts, accounting, spreadsheet generation. These do not depend on the individuality of the person doing them.
2. **Nuanced, relationship-driven tasks** (humans preserve): In-person engagement, storytelling, community relationships, understanding local context.

## Receipt Processing

Scan receipts with phone camera, drag photos into Claude, get a geotagged spreadsheet instantly. Phone metadata provides GPS coordinates for each purchase. Replaced manual Garmin GPS + spreadsheet workflow.

## Grant Writing (Core Use Case)

- Training Claude to write in Clayton's voice since 2019
- Iterative feedback loop:
  1. Give Claude a prompt with detailed context (sometimes 40 minutes of brain-dumping text)
  2. AI produces a draft
  3. Clayton edits it: "This is terrible. We would never talk like this."
  4. Returns the corrected version to Claude with five examples of grants Clayton wrote himself
  5. Says: "Write like this next time."
  6. Repeat for over a year
- Result: dramatically increased grant output volume and speed
- Key insight: "Garbage in, garbage out. The more you tell the AI before you hit enter, the better the output."

## EPA $4.7M Grant Experiment

- Deliberately used AI end-to-end for a competitive EPA environmental education grant
- Wanted to test: Can the federal government detect AI-generated submissions?
- Competed against National Wildlife Federation, Sierra Club, WWF
- Made it to the top 10 out of thousands of applicants
- EPA requested a follow-up call even though IDEAS for Us was not selected
- Conclusion: legislation has not caught up to the technology

## Key Risk

"AI in its best sense is a team member and in its worst sense is an adversary. What we don't want to do is create systems that remove the heart and soul of what we ultimately wanted to do. If we allow our uses of technology to erode that, then all of our work is ultimately for nothing."
