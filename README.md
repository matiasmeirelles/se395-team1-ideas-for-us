### How They Use AI

**Decision Framework:** Separate tasks into two categories:
1. **Utilitarian tasks** (AI handles): Data entry, receipts, accounting, spreadsheet generation. These do not depend on the individuality of the person doing them.
2. **Nuanced, relationship-driven tasks** (humans preserve): In-person engagement, storytelling, community relationships, understanding local context.

**Receipt Processing:** Scan receipts with phone camera, drag photos into Claude, get a geotagged spreadsheet instantly. Phone metadata provides GPS coordinates for each purchase. Replaced manual Garmin GPS + spreadsheet workflow.

**Grant Writing (Core Use Case):**
- Training Claude to write in Clayton's voice since 2019
- Iterative feedback loop:
  1. Give Claude a prompt with detailed context (sometimes 40 minutes of brain-dumping text)
  2. AI produces a draft
  3. Clayton edits it: "This is terrible. We would never talk like this."
  4. Returns the corrected version to Claude with five examples of grants Clayton wrote himself
  5. Says: "Write like this next time."
  6. Repeat for over a year
- Result: dramatically increased grant output volume and speed
- Key insight: "Garbage in, garbage out. The more you tell the AI before you hit enter, the better the output."

**EPA $4.7M Grant Experiment:**
- Deliberately used AI end-to-end for a competitive EPA environmental education grant
- Wanted to test: Can the federal government detect AI-generated submissions? Do they have metadata scanners?
- Competed against National Wildlife Federation, Sierra Club, WWF
- Made it to the top 10 out of thousands of applicants
- EPA requested a follow-up call even though IDEAS for Us was not selected
- Conclusion: legislation has not caught up to the technology

**Claude Corps (Anthropic Partnership):**
- IDEAS for Us was fast-listed for Cohort 1 of Anthropic's Claude Corps program (50 nonprofits)
- Moved to Cohort 2 due to budget timing. Starts November 2026
- Anthropic's team will work directly with the organization on internal AI structures

### Key Risk Clayton Identified

"AI in its best sense is a team member and in its worst sense is an adversary. What we don't want to do is create systems that remove the heart and soul of what we ultimately wanted to do. If we allow our uses of technology to erode that, then all of our work is ultimately for nothing."

### Proposed Student Project Areas

1. **Hardee County Biochar Project:** Agricultural project using dead citrus trees (killed by citrus greening disease) to create biochar. Hardee County lost 90%+ of citrus production. Student team would work on data curation, storytelling, grant case-building, political network mapping (senators, representatives, governor's office). Long-term project extending into 2027.
2. **Claude Corps / Anthropic Partnership:** Student invited to participate directly in Cohort 2 (starts November). Working with Anthropic's team on internal AI structures. Potential trip to California conference. High-access, high-visibility opportunity.
3. **Winter Park Urban Sustainability:** Focus on the Maitland side of Winter Park (neglected compared to Park Avenue side). Tree canopy, complete streets, bike trails, pocket parks. Orlando mayoral power vacuum creating upheaval in city governance. AI proposed to analyze town hall meeting minutes, voting records, commissioner and committee data. Goal: accelerate strategic decision-making in an uncertain political environment.

### Other Programs Referenced

- **Fleet Farming:** Urban agriculture program using bicycles, farmlets on front lawns, solar DJ. Hundreds of young people participate. Example of differentiation through concept design, not just AI output.
- **Matuvo Paul (Uganda):** Invented a stacked soil tower compacting 300 yards of soil with a compost chute and worm castings system. Funded by an IDEAS for Us grant. Scaled to thousands of units across the Buduta District. Feeds communities in extreme poverty.
- **Winter Park Land Trust:** Clayton is a founding member. Raised $3M internally, matched by city for $6M. Purchased land that became Seven Oaks Park.

### Key Quotes

On standing out as AI levels the playing field:
"It's one thing to hit enter and get a farm program. It's another thing to create an urban agricultural program that uses people on bicycles riding from farm to farm with farmlets on front lawns and a solar DJ creating a whole fun experience. That detail is what happens as a unique identifying aspect."

On pitching without a portfolio:
"The first clients are the hardest. Sell the dream. Focus on the ROI. Show them examples of work you didn't do but that represents the outcome you can deliver. Even if you have to take the first client at a dirt cheap price, winning that first yes is what matters."

On the nonprofit sector and AI:
"When the Edith Bush Institute here at Rollins was asked about AI in grant writing, their stance was: use it, see what happens. Most agencies and foundations have no AI policies yet."
